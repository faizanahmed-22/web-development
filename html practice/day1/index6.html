INDIA

INDIA, officially the Republic of India,[j][20] is a country in South Asia. It is the world's seventh-largest country by area and its most populous.[21] Bounded by the Indian Ocean on the south, the Arabian Sea on the southwest, and the Bay of Bengal on the southeast, it shares land borders with Pakistan to the west,[k] China, Nepal, and Bhutan to the north, and Bangladesh and Myanmar to the east. In the Indian Ocean, India is near Sri Lanka and the Maldives.[l] Modern humans arrived on the Indian subcontinent from Africa by 55,000 years ago.[23] Settled life emerged in the western margins of the Indus river basin 9,000 years ago, evolving into the Indus Valley Civilisation of the third millennium BCE.[24] By 1200 BCE, an archaic form of Sanskrit, an Indo-European language, diffused into India from the northwest,[25] its earliest hymns containing elements of what would become Hinduism.[26] The pre-existing Dravidian and other languages were gradually superseded in India's northern plains.[27] Across the central stretch of these plains, rapid urbanisation took place, and after 500 BCE a caste-based, hereditary form of social stratification began to solidify as Hinduism took fuller shape.[28] During this period, Buddhism and Jainism arose, proclaiming social orders unlinked to heredity.[29] The subsequent emergence of the loose-knit Maurya and Gupta Empires[30] gave rise to wide-ranging cultural and scientific creativity.[31] However, these eras saw a steady decline in women's status,[32] alongside the eventual institutionalisation of untouchability.[33] In South India, across the first millennium CE, the Middle kingdoms transmitted Dravidian language scripts and cultural traditions to Southeast Asia.[34] Well before the millennium's end, Judaism, Christianity, Islam, and Zoroastrianism had become established on India's southern and western coasts.[35] Early in the 2nd millennium, Muslim armies from Central Asia intermittently overran India's northern plains,[36] eventually establishing the Delhi Sultanate and drawing India into the cosmopolitan networks of medieval Islam.[37] The Vijayanagara Empire built a powerful military-fiscal state that fostered a flourishing, distinctively South Indian Hindu culture.[38] During this era, Sikhism emerged in the Punjab, promoting an egalitarian devotion that transcended traditional Hindu and Muslim boundaries.[39] The Mughal Empire ushered in two centuries of economic expansion and relative peace,[40] leaving a lasting legacy of administrative centralisation[41] and cultural synthesis.[42] The expanding rule of the British East India Company consolidated territorial control over the subcontinent[43] while transforming India into a colonial economy.[44] Crown rule, or the British Raj, began in 1858.[45] Although equality before the law promised to Indians was slow to materialise,[46][47] both British India and, to a lesser extent, the princely states[48] saw technological changes[49] as modern education and more organised public life took root.[50][51] A nationalist movement emerged in India, the first in the non-European British Empire.[52][53] Noted for nonviolent resistance after 1920,[54] it was a major factor in ending British rule.[55] In 1947, the British Indian Empire was partitioned into the independent dominions of a Hindu-majority India and a Muslim-majority Pakistan. The partition brought large-scale loss of life and unprecedented migration.[56]
